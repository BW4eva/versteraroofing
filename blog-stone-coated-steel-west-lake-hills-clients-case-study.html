Metal Roof Case Study: How a $3M West Lake Hills Home Almost Lost $80K Over a 'Cheap' Roof

Published: 5 May 2025

A client recently asked us the question we get so often, "Why do you recommend this expensive roof over this more economical one?"

This question came mid-conversation as we were consulting about a large tile roof repair on her West Lake Hills home. She was weighing two replacement options: a designer shingle roof or a luxury metal shingle system.

"Before answering, we asked a simple question in response: "What is your goal here? Is it:

  1. Desired longevity?
  2. Desired savings?
  3. Curb appeal?
  4. Maintenance budget?"

An average shingle roof, the economical option, costs between $45-50k for this home, but the lifespan is 10-15 years, has low backend efficiencies, lowers curb appeal from her existing roof type, and has a maintenance budget of $500/year if it lasts 10 years.

Our client planned to live in the home for another 30-40 years, wanted the roof to recoup costs over time, increase curb appeal, and have a virtually $0 maintenance budget.

So we recommended a metal shingle roof called stone coated steel to solve those problems for her. Here are the numbers:

  1. Stone coated steel lasts 50-70 years ($150,000-$350,000 saved in shingle roof replacements).
  2. Stone coated steel saves between 15-20% in cooling bills due to UV and insulation technology saving 10-20% on most insurance policy premiums due to its wind and hail resistance ratings. (On average saving $600-700 in HVAC and $700-1000 in insurance premiums each year or $65,000 to $119,000 over the life of the roof).
  3. Metal shingle roofs increase home value from curb appeal and functionality 1.5-3% or $45,000-$90,000.
  4. Maintenance on stone coated steel is negligible (maybe $500 every 4-5 years to re-seal flashing).

Key takeaway: disregarding all but longevity, by year 10-15, the homeowner will be well over $80,000 ahead by investing in the higher end product, and by the time the roof is eventually replaced, will be nearing $500,000 in savings.
