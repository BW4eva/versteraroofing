Hail & Storm Damage

How do I know if my roof has hail damage?

Look for three things together: random circular impact marks on the shingles, soft or spongy bruises in the shingle, and matching dents on nearby soft metal like gutters, vents, and HVAC fins. Any one of these on its own can be ambiguous. All three together tell a clear, defensible story to an insurance adjuster.

The catch is that most hail damage can't be seen from the driveway. Hail doesn't just knock granules loose; it fractures the fiberglass mat beneath the surface, leaving a weak spot that looks fine from the ground. That's why we provide free, photo-documented inspections with a written report, so you know whether a claim makes sense before you call your insurer.

What are the signs of hail damage on a roof?

Here at Verstera Roofing, there are 11 signs we look for, grouped by where you'll find them:

On the shingles

  1. Bald spots — round or irregular areas where granules were knocked away, exposing asphalt.
  2. Soft bruises — a spongy feel under gentle pressure, meaning the mat beneath has fractured.
  3. Fresh, dark, shiny asphalt — rather than the gray, chalky look of age-related granule loss.
  4. Random distribution — impact marks scattered across storm-facing slopes, not in lines or along foot-traffic paths.

On gutters and soft metal

  1. Dented gutters and granules packed at downspout exits.
  2. Dimpled vents, chimney caps, and HVAC fins.
  3. Damaged skylight frames, window screens, and painted metal on the exterior.

Around the roof structure and inside the attic

  1. Missing or cracked shingles and damaged ridge caps.
  2. Lifted or separated flashing along walls, chimneys, skylights, and valleys.
  3. Daylight visible through the roof deck from inside the attic.
  4. Wet or compressed insulation, ceiling stains, or active drips in the attic.

Dented gutters matter more than most homeowners realize. Soft aluminum doesn't wear in ways that mimic impact damage, so a fresh circular dent is hard to explain any other way. When suspected hail marks on shingles and dented gutters show up together, that combination carries serious weight with an adjuster. Signs 10 and 11 are the most urgent: interior water means the roof is already failing to shed water.

What size hail damages a roof?

Hail 1 inch or larger is where functional damage becomes likely. Below 1 inch, damage on a newer roof in good condition is usually cosmetic. Above 1 inch, expect compromised waterproofing, cracked seal strips, and fractured shingle mats. At 1.5 to 2 inches, widespread bruising and broken adhesive seals are common across the whole roof.

According to NOAA storm records, Central Texas and the area around Austin averages more than 4 days per year with hail at or above the 1-inch severe threshold.

Roof age matters too: a 10-year-old shingle in marginal condition behaves very differently under quarter-sized hail than a newly installed Class 4 impact-resistant system.

How can I tell hail damage from normal wear, wind damage, or a manufacturing defect?

Hail damage is sudden and random; the other causes follow a pattern. Adjusters are trained to tell the difference, so it helps to know what they're looking for:

  • Normal wear and aging develops slowly across the whole roof, worst on sun-exposed slopes: broad granule loss, fading, curling edges, and algae streaks. Exposed asphalt looks gray and oxidized, and there are no concave dents or soft spots.
  • Wind damage concentrates where pressure is highest — ridges, edges, corners, and eaves — as lifted, creased, or missing shingles that follow the storm's direction. A severe Central Texas storm can cause both wind and hail damage, so check for both.
  • Manufacturing defects show up as bare spots, blisters, or discoloration repeating in similar positions across many shingles. There's no soft mat underneath and no matching dents on gutters or vents, which is usually the clearest giveaway.

Is it safe to inspect my roof myself after a hailstorm?

Inspect from the ground, not from the roof. Hail bruising can weaken shingles in ways you can't see, and a wet or damaged surface is a serious fall hazard. Leave climbing to a qualified professional with proper equipment.

A safe ground-level check looks like this:

  1. Wait for the storm to fully pass, then check for downed power lines (treat every one as live) and hanging branches or debris before approaching the house.
  2. Walk all four sides of the house, scanning each slope with binoculars or your phone's zoom for missing shingles, damaged ridge caps, and lifted flashing.
  3. Check the metal and exterior surfaces — gutters, downspouts, vents, HVAC units, window screens, siding, and painted metal — for dents and impact marks.
  4. If it's safe to reach, check the attic for daylight through the deck, wet insulation, stains, or drips. Photograph everything before touching anything.

Call a professional right away if you see sagging in the roofline, active interior leaks, or large sections of missing shingles.

How soon after a storm should I get inspected?

As soon as it's safe. Insurance policies limit how long you have to file (see filing deadlines below), and early documentation makes it much harder for damage to be disputed as old wear or neglect. Damage also rarely stays still: by the time a leak shows up inside, functional damage has often been there for weeks.

How often should I get my roof inspected?

Every 2–3 years, and after every major hail or wind storm. Regular inspections give you photo-documented evidence of your roof's condition, so if a storm does cause damage, you can show what changed and file a claim with confidence.

Insurance Claims

Should I call my insurance company or a roofer first?

Either order works. Some homeowners start with a roofer to get a free, photo-documented inspection and confirm whether the damage is worth a claim. Others call their insurer first and use contractors from its network.

Both are fine as long as you work with people you trust. Make sure your roofer gives you clear photo evidence, and make sure any contractor your insurer recommends is chosen for quality, not just lowest cost.

How long do I have to file a roof damage claim in Texas?

Many Texas homeowner policies give you one year from the storm date, and some allow as little as 90 days. Check the "notice of loss" language in your own policy, and see the Texas Department of Insurance website for state-level guidance. Waiting a full season to act can close that window for good, no matter how clear the damage is.

How do I document hail damage for my insurance claim?

Photograph everything before any repairs or cleanup, then back it up with storm records and a timeline. An adjuster needs to confirm three things: a covered storm happened, the damage is real and storm-related, and it isn't pre-existing wear. Your documentation should prove all three.

  • Wide shots of each side of the house showing which slopes are affected.
  • Medium shots of each damaged area in context.
  • Close-ups of individual impacts with a ruler or coin for scale.
  • A narrated video walking the perimeter and naming each area and type of damage.
  • Supporting evidence: granules in gutters, dented vents and metal, and any interior staining.
  • Storm records from the National Weather Service or NOAA confirming the date and hail size in your area.
  • Paperwork: prior inspection reports, maintenance invoices, and receipts for any emergency tarping.
  • A timeline: the storm date, when you found the damage, and every step you took afterwards.

What should I avoid doing before the adjuster visits?

Don't make permanent repairs. Tarp openings only to stop active leaks, and photograph the tarp before and after it goes on. Don't throw away damaged materials, granule-filled debris, or any hailstones you still have. And don't rely on a contractor's verbal assessment alone — get photographed and written findings that tie the damage to the storm date.

Can you meet my insurance adjuster for me?

Yes. We meet the adjuster at your property to make sure every bit of damage is documented. This helps protect you from a partial or underpaid claim.

How much hail damage does insurance need to approve a full roof replacement?

Adjusters count qualifying hail impacts in 10-by-10-foot "test squares" on each slope. In Texas, roughly 8 to 10 impacts per test square is a common benchmark for full replacement, though some insurers use as few as 5. Thresholds vary by insurer, and this kind of count can only be done by someone who physically walks and measures the roof.

How long does the insurance claim process take?

Most storm-damage claims take 7–14 days to get an adjuster appointment and 1–3 weeks for approval. Once approved, we can usually schedule your replacement within a few days, assuming your desired material available.

Costs, Deductibles & Financing

How much does a new roof cost in Texas?

For asphalt shingles in Central Texas, replacement typically runs $10,000–$25,000 for small to average homes (as of summer 2025). Premium materials like metal, stone coated steel, or tile cost more upfront but can save significantly over the life of the roof.

If the replacement is covered by insurance, your out-of-pocket cost is usually just your deductible.

Do I have to pay my deductible?

Yes. Texas law (HB 2102) requires homeowners to pay their deductible. Any contractor who "waives" or "absorbs" it is violating state law. We provide the proper paperwork (Certificate of Completion, RCV/ACV documentation, and so on) so your claim and deductible are handled legally.

Why are some roofers offering to "pay my deductible"?

Because they're breaking the law to win your business. Texas HB 2102 prohibits deductible waiving, and these offers often go hand in hand with fraudulent claims, low-grade materials, and poor workmanship.

What is the difference between a roof repair and a replacement?

A repair fixes a localized problem; a replacement is for widespread damage. Repairs handle things like a leak or a few missing shingles. Replacement makes sense when damage is spread across the roof or when patching would compromise its integrity. After a storm, insurance typically covers full replacement once damage meets the insurer's test-square threshold.

How long does it take to replace a roof?

Most asphalt shingle residential roofs are completed in one day. Larger or specialty roofs (metal, tile, steep pitch) may take 2–3 days.

Roof Maintenance in Central Texas

How often should I maintain my roof in Central Texas?

At least once a year, ideally in early winter — after the autumn leaves have fallen and before the spring rainy season. We recommend these five tasks every year:

  1. Check shingle condition — look for granule loss from heat and age, nail pops (nails working back up through the shingle above, which can cause leaks), and signs of hail or wind damage. If you're not sure what you're looking at, schedule an inspection and we'll help.
  2. Clear debris from the roof, especially the valleys — sticks and leaves decay and build up, blocking runoff and causing water to pool. Shingles are water-shedding, not waterproof, so pooled water almost always leads to leaks.
  3. Re-seal around vents, accessories, and penetrations — skylights, vents, and pipes are all potential leak points. Across thousands of inspections, we've found that even roof-approved sealant dry-rots every 3–5 years in Central Texas heat, often causing leaks right below pipe jacks and vents. Re-sealing costs about $10 and 10 minutes and can save hundreds or thousands in repairs.
  4. Seal exposed or rusty nail heads — exposed nails rust and let water seep into the attic. A dab of roof-approved sealant prevents a small problem from becoming a serious one.
  5. Clean your gutters — clogged gutters overflow, get heavy, and cause costly fascia rot. Replacing fascia is expensive; cleaning gutters isn't.

Be careful. If you're not comfortable on the roof or a ladder, book a maintenance appointment and we'll handle it.
